How to Store Supplements So They Last

Most supplement bottles carry storage advice in small print, and most buyers ignore it. That is understandable, but it is also wasteful. Heat, light, moisture and air all change what is inside a capsule or tablet over time, and a product stored badly may lose potency long before its expiry date. Good storage is simple, and it protects both the product and the money spent on it.

Heat is the first enemy. Kitchens look convenient, but a cupboard above a cooker or next to a kettle can get warm many times a day. Repeated warming speeds up chemical breakdown, especially for oils, probiotics and plant extracts. A cool, steady room is better than a warm kitchen, and a bedroom drawer often beats a kitchen cupboard.

Light is the second enemy. Clear bottles look attractive, but sunlight breaks down sensitive ingredients. Amber or opaque bottles exist for a reason. Keep bottles out of direct sunlight, and do not leave them on a windowsill, even for a few days. If a bottle is clear, store it inside a closed cupboard.

Moisture is the third enemy, and bathrooms are the worst place for supplements. Steam from showers raises humidity, and damp air can soften tablets, clump powders and encourage mould. A dry cupboard in a bedroom or hallway is a far better home. Never transfer tablets to a bathroom cabinet just because it is tidy.

Air matters too. Every time a bottle is opened, fresh oxygen enters. That is unavoidable, but leaving a bottle open on a counter for hours is not. Close lids promptly, and do not decant products into decorative jars that do not seal properly. The original bottle, with its seal and desiccant, is usually the best container.

Finally, keep a simple system. Note the date you open a bottle, keep it away from children and pets, and check expiry dates before use rather than after.
